    Five things that matter most

    If you remember only five things from this guide, remember these.

    1. Blackboard is the single place for your course. Materials, announcements, assignments and the Teams meeting link all go into the Blackboard course.
    2. The syllabus is a binding agreement with students. Follow the content and assessment rules it sets out; assessment rules are not changed during the semester.
    3. Cancel classes only in an emergency. Always notify the Head of Department and the Teaching Organisation Office (planowanie@kozminski.edu.pl), and inform students immediately via the Blackboard course.
    4. Enter grades on time. If you cannot, tell students why and when they can expect their results.
    5. Archive student work and complete the Mała pętla (Small Loop). Quality procedures depend on records that you create.

    Blackboard – the main teaching platform

    From 2026/2027, all classes in degree programmes are run in Blackboard – regardless of degree programme, level or mode of study.

    Microsoft Teams – work with second- and third-year Bachelor's groups

    2026/2027

    Blackboard – main course environment for all years, levels and modes of study

    Microsoft Teams – tool for live sessions

    Important

    Please use Blackboard even if you are more used to Teams. If some academic teachers use Blackboard and others use Teams, students get confused about where to find materials and information.

    Which tool for what

    Blackboard as the main course environment, Teams for live sessions – this combination works best.

    Blackboard

    Main course environment

    • materials, assignments, instructions, deadlines
    • ongoing course announcements
    • e-learning activities
    • Teams meeting link(s)
    Microsoft Teams

    Tool for live sessions

    • running online meetings and online classes
    • A meeting link on Microsoft Teams is created by the academic teacher and linked to the Blackboard course.
    Good to know

    The University does not currently have a licence for Blackboard’s built-in live-session tools, so Microsoft Teams remains the platform used for synchronous classes. Work is underway to automatically add Teams links to Blackboard courses, making the transition from Blackboard to Teams as seamless as possible.

    Blackboard course – requirements

    The course contains all the materials and information students need to complete the course.

    • Every course has a course on the Blackboard platform.
    • Teaching materials (presentations, files, and links) are shared exclusively via Blackboard.
    • Communication with students and sharing organisational information – via Blackboard.
    • E-learning activities (assignments, tests, asynchronous materials) take place in this environment.
    • Blackboard supports on-campus, online and blended classes.

    Accessing and preparing your Blackboard course

    Do this now, even if your classes start later.

    1. Open BlackboardmyKozminski → “Application Catalogue” → Blackboard tile.
    2. Log inWith your “University account” – the same as for other ALK systems.
    3. Check your coursesMake sure all your courses are there and check the student lists if students have already been enrolled in the course.
    4. Open the courseOpen it for students and add your teaching materials before the first class.
    Warning

    Blackboard courses are closed by default. If you do not open your course, students cannot see it.

    Tip

    Timetable planning is still being finalised, and groups are synchronised with Blackboard on an ongoing basis. If a course, group or student is missing, report it to e-learning@kozminski.edu.pl. See also Common issues.

    Communication with students

    Blackboard is the main communication channel. You decide which other channels you use – and you should tell students clearly.

    Main channel

    Blackboard messages

    You can see which course the student is writing from. Announcements and organisational changes also go through Blackboard.

    Optional

    E-mail

    Acceptable if you prefer it – but you do not have to accept e-mail if you announce other channels.

    Remember

    A simple rule works: “Materials and messages are in Blackboard.” Announce your channels at the first class and in writing in the Blackboard course.

    Online classes in Teams – 4 steps

    Schedule meetings in advance for the whole semester: one fixed link or separate meetings for individual sessions.

    1. New meetingTeams → Calendar → New meeting. Name the meeting, e.g. “Course XYZ – online classes”.
    2. First sessionSet the first session and save. For regular classes: “Repeat” → e.g. weekly.
    3. Copy linkOpen the saved meeting and select “Copy meeting link”.
    4. Link in BlackboardPaste it in a visible place in the course, e.g. “Online classes – Teams link”.
    Tip

    The same link can be reused for subsequent classes – there is no need to create a new meeting each time.

    Part IV · AI in your courses

    Use of AI: the rules

    You decide how students may use AI in your course. Communicate the decision clearly, orally and in writing, so that you can refer to it later.

    AI policy for students

    Available in the “Kształcenie w ALK” team in Microsoft Teams. The rules for students are the same as last year.

    Your decision

    Under the Rector's regulation on generative AI, the academic teacher decides the scope of its use. If you choose a course without AI, justify it by the type of classes and verify learning in class (e.g. in-class writing and discussion), consult the ban with the Vice-dean.

    Independent work

    Students must work independently and label any use of AI as required by the AI policy and your guidelines.

    Setting your AI use guidelines

    Four steps that give you a clear basis for assessing student work.

    1. Decide what is allowedDefine in which tasks students may use AI (e.g. research) and how AI use must be labelled.
    2. Write it in the syllabusUse the remarks section. If your syllabus has already been approved, editing it is difficult – then publish the guidelines in writing in Blackboard.
    3. Present it at the first classMake it part of the agreement with students and publish it in the Blackboard course as an important document.
    4. Refer to it when assessingWritten guidelines are your basis for not accepting work, e.g. when AI use was not labelled or the work did not follow the guidelines.

    Ban AI or define the scope?

    A ban is your right. In practice, defining the scope and assessing the process is usually more effective.

    Recommended

    • Show students how they may use AI and teach them to use it ethically – they will use it at work.
    • Assess the process, not only the final product: e.g. drafts, discussion of each chapter, presentations of work in progress.
    • If you choose a course without AI, justify it by the type of classes and verify learning in class (e.g. in-class writing and discussion). Total AI ban should be consulted with a Vice-dean.

    Be aware

    • A complete ban is hard to enforce: AI is built into research databases, search engines and other tools students use.
    • AI detectors do not give 100% certainty. Do not base a decision on a detector result alone.

    When you suspect AI misuse

    Unauthorised or unlabelled AI use is treated in the same way as plagiarism: the work (or the relevant part of it) is not accepted. The student must be able to show that the work is their own.

    What you can rely on

    The student's ability – or inability – to explain and defend the work in a conversation with you, e.g. by answering simple questions about it.

    Process requirements that were not met, e.g. chapters that were never discussed with you.

    Missing AI labelling, contrary to the guidelines you published.

    Verifiable facts about the work, such as how and when the document was created.

    Remember

    If a student does not follow the AI policy and cannot defend their work in discussion with you, you have the right to award a failing grade.

    Part V · Assessment and examinations

    The Examination and E-learning Centre and Inspera

    Examinations held during the examination period are organised with the Examination and E-learning Centre, so that every student takes the exam under the same conditions.

    Exam support

    If staff of the Examination and E-learning Centre attend your exam, they are there to support you and the students – not to check on you.

    Graded credits

    Involving the Examination and E-learning Centre is not obligatory, but you may invite it to help.

    Important

    If you plan to administer an Inspera exam yourself, notify the Examination and E-learning Centre in advance: egzaminy@kozminski.edu.pl.

    Grading and grade entry

    Grades are entered in myKozminski → “Teaching Classes”.

    14 daysto enter grades after the first attempt
    7 daysto enter grades after the second attempt
    • Absence from the first attempt does not result in a failing grade; the student may take the second attempt.
    • A passing grade is final and cannot be improved by retaking the assessment.
    Late? Communicate

    If marking takes longer, tell students why and when they can expect their results – they have the right to this information. Students who hear from their academic teacher wait calmly; those who hear nothing often escalate, sometimes as far as the Rector.

    Study Regulations effective from 1 October 2026 New

    For academic teachers, the most important change concerns the Master's thesis seminar.

    2024 Regulations

    No separate provision on a failing grade for not submitting the thesis by the first deadline. After the final deadline: removal from the student register.

    In practice, Colleges handled this differently: some academic teachers entered “nb”, others a failing grade.

    2026 Regulations · 37(2)–(3)

    Failure to submit the thesis means a failing grade for the seminar at both the first attempt and the resit.

    Unchanged

    Thesis submission deadlines remain unchanged: 20/31 January and 15 June/15 July ( 37(1)).

    Archiving work: paper-based academic submissions and oral examinations

    Paper-based academic submissions

    Staff academic teachers

    Paper-based academic submissions are kept in the Department, as before.

    Non-staff academic teachers

    Hand them over to the Quality Office (room A 108), labelled according to the template: Form for Identification of submitted paper students’ work . The Quality Office passes them on to the archives. This also applies if your Department has no space to store the work.

    Keep the examination questions and examination notes as a record of how the exam was conducted – in the same way as for thesis defence records. The accreditation committee may ask for these too.

    Staff academic teachers

    Keep them in the Department, like paper-based academic submissions.

    Non-staff academic teachers

    Hand them over to the Quality Office (room A 108).

    After the examination period, reflect on your course: how did your students do, and were the learning outcomes achieved? The Small Loop is currently open.

    Evaluation surveys: the new approach New

    Response rates in the previous system were very low. After a pilot last semester, surveys are still completed electronically, but are now presented in class by survey administrators.

    What to expect

    You will be informed in advance. Survey administrators will introduce themselves and explain why they are there. Please let them in.

    Timing

    Because of the logistics, it cannot be guaranteed that they will always come at the start or at the end of a class.

    Why it matters

    Collecting student feedback is a statutory requirement.
